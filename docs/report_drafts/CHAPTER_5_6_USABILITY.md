## System Usability Scale

The ten SUS items were scored as specified by Brooke (38): the contribution of
items 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9 is the scale position minus one, that of items 2, 4, 6,
8 and 10 is five minus the scale position, and the total is multiplied by 2.5.
One participant left a single item blank; following Brooke's instruction that
an unanswerable item should be recorded at the centre point, it was treated as
neutral rather than excluding the response. Excluding that participant instead
gives a mean of 71.94 (n = 49, SD 11.17), so the choice does not affect the
result materially.

**Table 5.8 — SUS results and comparison with educational technology (39).**

| | Mean | SD | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| **U-Pal** | **71.80** | **11.10** | **50** |
| All educational technology | 70.09 | 12.98 | 170 surveys |
| Multimedia | 76.43 | 9.45 | |
| Mobile applications | 73.62 | 13.49 | |
| Affective tutoring systems | 68.87 | 7.30 | |
| Internet platforms | 66.25 | 12.42 | |
| University websites | 63.82 | 16.52 | |

U-Pal scored a mean of 71.80 (SD 11.10, median 72.50, range 42.5–100), with a
95% confidence interval of 68.72 to 74.88. Against the systematic review of 104
studies by Vlachogianni and Tselios (39), this places U-Pal above the mean for
university websites (63.82) and internet platforms (66.25), both of which fall
below the lower bound of the confidence interval. The comparison with the
overall educational technology mean of 70.09 is different in kind: U-Pal is
1.71 points higher, but 70.09 lies inside the confidence interval, so the
result is consistent with that benchmark rather than significantly above it.
The honest reading is that U-Pal achieves usability typical of educational
technology generally, and better than the specific category — university
websites — that it most directly replaces.

One caveat from the same review applies here. Vlachogianni and Tselios found
that the number of participants correlates negatively with the reported SUS
score (r = −0.259, p = 0.001), meaning smaller studies tend to report higher
usability. At n = 50 this sample sits above the median study size in their
corpus, so the score is unlikely to be inflated by that effect.

| Group | n | Mean SUS | SD |

| First language Welsh | 4 | 88.75 | 9.68 |
| Beginner | 24 | 71.77 | 8.39 |

| Year 3 | 19 | 74.74 | 12.19 |
| Postgraduate | 8 | 72.50 | 5.98 |
| Year 1 | 12 | 63.54 | 11.15 |

First-language Welsh speakers rated the system highest by a wide margin, at
88.75 against 71.80 overall. This is the group the system was built for, and
the direction of the result is encouraging, but with n = 4 it is indicative
only and no statistical claim is made from it. A second pattern is more robust:
Year 1 students scored the system markedly lower (63.54) than Year 2 (75.23)
and Year 3 (74.74), which may reflect that first-year students have less
knowledge of university services against which to judge the answers.
Participants who use chatbots daily scored U-Pal lowest of any usage group
(66.79, n = 7), while occasional users scored it highest, suggesting that
frequent users of commercial assistants apply a higher standard.

## Bilingual and trust items

**Table 5.10 — Bilingual and trust items (1–5 Likert, n = 50).**

| Item | Mean | Agree or strongly agree |
|---|---|---|
| English responses were clear and easy to understand | 4.44 | 49 (98%) |
| Welsh responses were clear and easy to understand | 3.98 | 40 (80%) |
| Switching between Welsh and English felt natural | 3.84 | 37 (74%) |
| I would trust U-Pal's answers about university services | 3.58 | 32 (64%) |
| I would recommend U-Pal to another UWTSD student | 3.72 | 34 (68%) |
| I would use U-Pal before contacting a human support officer | 3.22 | 20 (40%) |

The gap between English clarity (4.44) and Welsh clarity (3.98) mirrors the
asymmetry measured throughout this chapter: the Welsh experience is rated
positively but consistently below the English one. The lowest-scoring item is
the willingness to use U-Pal in preference to a human support officer, endorsed
by only 40% of participants. That is a reasonable position for students to hold
about an unofficial prototype, and it is consistent with the 64% who said they
would trust its answers; students appear to regard the system as a useful first
port of call rather than a replacement for staff.

## Open-ended responses

Forty-four participants answered at least one open question. Three themes
recur.

The bilingual capability is the most frequently praised feature, named in the
majority of positive responses — "the ability to switch between two languages
seamlessly", "Welsh language detection is quite good and response exceeded my
expectations", "first Welsh chatbot that can carry human-like conversation".

Knowledge coverage and currency is the most common criticism, with participants
reporting that the system "lacks a little information", that "the data seems
old", and that it "needs improvement from the university information". This
corroborates the retrieval limitations reported in 5.1 from the user side.

One response provides independent evidence of the bilingual content asymmetry
identified in 5.1. A participant noticed that the Welsh answer to a library
question stated a six-book borrowing limit that the English answer omitted.
That is a content divergence between the two language indexes, observed by a
user rather than by a benchmark, and it supports the argument that the Welsh
and English corpora are not equivalent in depth or content.

Two further suggestions recur without being defects: requests for additional
languages beyond Welsh and English, and for integration with university systems
such as Moodle, Teams and application status. A smaller group asked for
onboarding guidance, noting that less technically confident users would benefit
from a short demonstration of how to interact with the system. These are
returned to in 6.4.
